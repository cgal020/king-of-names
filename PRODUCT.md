# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Primary: Cameron, a company owner who meets hundreds of people a year across Dubai, Australia, Thailand and elsewhere. Typical moment of use: standing in a bar, lobby or event right after a handshake, phone in one hand, maybe a drink in the other, with seconds before the next conversation. Second moment: in a city again months later, opening the map to remember who he knows there before a meeting.

Secondary: a handful of people Cameron invites, each with a private account.

## Product Purpose

Remember everyone you meet. A five-second voice note becomes a profile stamped with place and date; years later the name and the one detail that matters are a search or a map tap away. Success is Cameron greeting someone by name and context he would otherwise have lost. Speed of capture is the product.

## Brand Personality

Quiet, quick, discreet. A quiet utility in the spirit of Apple Notes or Things: light, clean, fast, and out of the way. Calm and premium through restraint, not decoration. The voice is plain and brief ("Who did you just meet?"), never cheerful or salesy.

## Anti-references

- Corporate CRM (Salesforce, HubSpot): forms full of fields, pipelines, stages, lead scores, dashboards. People here are people remembered, not leads.
- Anything that makes a private memory aid look like a sales tool or a social network.

## Design Principles

1. Capture beats everything. The record button is always one tap away and nothing competes with it on the home screen.
2. One job per screen, reachable by thumb. Primary actions sit in the lower half; reading happens in the top half.
3. Names first. A person's name is the largest thing on any screen that shows them; context (where, when, one line of notes) follows.
4. Fix, don't fill. Review screens are for glancing and correcting, so empty fields stay quiet instead of demanding input.
5. Discreet by default. This holds personal details about people who do not know they are in it; nothing is showy, shareable or public-looking.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Tap targets at least 44 px. Legible in a dim bar and in bright sunlight, in light and dark mode following the system. Respect reduced motion. Never use colour alone to signal state (low-confidence names also get a text hint). Names from many languages and scripts must render and search correctly.
