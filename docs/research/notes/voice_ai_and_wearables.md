# Voice-first AI note-capture apps and AI wearables used to remember people (as of October 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-07. Official pages were fetched on that date unless noted. Prices are USD unless noted. Some secondary sources (aggregators, SEO review sites) are flagged where used. The session's web-search budget ran out partway through, so several items were checked only by fetching known URLs. Gaps are listed explicitly.

## 1. Status in 2026: alive, acquired or discontinued, and what happened to users' data

### Takeaway
The standalone "always-listening memory pendant" category has consolidated into Big Tech. Meta bought Limitless (Dec 2025; pendant sales stopped, service shut off in the EU, UK and five other markets with a two-week export window). Amazon bought Bee (July 2025). Humane is dead (Feb 2025; all data deleted). Rabbit survives as a niche agent gadget. Plaud and Omi are the main independent recorder and pendant players still selling. Software-only voice-note apps (Voicenotes, AudioPen, Letterly, TalkNotes, Cleft, Superwhisper, Otter, Granola, Mem, Notion, Apple Notes) are all alive and adding AI chat, MCP and integrations.

### Cited Findings

- Existing customers are supported "throughout 2026," pay no subscription and "get the unlimited plan for free." — [limitless.ai](https://www.limitless.ai/)
- Service ended Dec 5, 2025 in Brazil, China, the EU, Israel, South Korea, Turkey and the UK. Users there had until Dec 19, 2025 to download their data before permanent deletion. — [limitless.ai](https://www.limitless.ai/); [TechInformed](https://techinformed.com/meta-acquires-limitless-pendant-users-moved-to-free-unlimited-plan/)
- The Rewind desktop app and the recording features in the web and desktop apps were wound down. Export and delete tools shipped in the app. — [TechInformed](https://techinformed.com/meta-acquires-limitless-pendant-users-moved-to-free-unlimited-plan/)
- Terms were not disclosed. The team joined Meta Reality Labs for "personal superintelligence" wearables. — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/meta-acquires-ai-wearables-start-up-limitless-as-it-doubles-down-on-personal-superintelligence); [TechCrunch, Dec 5 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/05/meta-acquires-ai-device-startup-limitless)

### Inferences
- Acquisitions in this category have hurt users. Limitless gave non-US users about two weeks to export, and Humane deleted everything after about 10 days. A new entrant can make durable data export and a published "if we shut down" policy part of its trust story.
- Big Tech (Meta, Amazon) is buying "ambient memory" to feed glasses and assistants. Expect the incumbents' memory features to land inside glasses or Alexa, not as standalone people-memory apps.

- The exact Limitless end-of-support date after "throughout 2026" is not published.

## 2. What each product does, platforms/hardware, capture trigger and time-to-capture

### Takeaway
Capture triggers fall into four patterns: (1) always-on ambient listening (Omi, Limitless, Friend, optionally Bee); (2) press-and-hold hardware buttons (Plaud NotePin S highlight, Bee voice note, Pebble Index 01 ring, Sandbar Stream ring); (3) wrist or OS shortcuts (Granola watch face, Cleft Action button, widgets and CarPlay, Voicenotes and Letterly watch apps); (4) wake word on glasses ("Hey Meta, remember…"). No vendor publishes a measured time-to-capture.

### Cited Findings
**Wearables**
- **Bee:** worn on the wrist or as a clip. TechCrunch's Jan 2026 hands-on says it is not always-on by default: a button press records, a green light shows recording, a double press bookmarks or processes, and press-and-hold makes a voice note or AI chat. — [TechCrunch hands-on](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/12/hands-on-with-bee-amazons-latest-ai-wearable/). This conflicts with earlier coverage saying it "continuously listens unless muted manually." — [PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/amazon-just-bought-the-50-wearable-that-never-stops-listening_id172547)
- **Bee** official specs: dual mics with noise filtering, "up to 7 days" battery, modular wristband plus clip, iOS, Android and Apple Watch apps. The page also says "Bee Pioneer currently supports iOS only," which is internally inconsistent. — [bee.computer](https://www.bee.computer/)
- **Omi:** always-on pendant (cord, clip or wristband). 10–14 h battery. Records online or offline and syncs later. Apps on iPhone, Android, Mac and web. — [omi.me](https://www.omi.me/)
- **Limitless Pendant (discontinued):** always-on aluminum clip (IP54). About 6–7 h of continuous recording in practice versus 100 h advertised standby. iOS 18+ and Android 13+. — [Fast.io review, Jun 2026](https://fast.io/resources/limitless-ai-review-2026/)
- **Plaud NotePin S:** press-and-hold "instant highlight" for quick voice notes. Records up to about 9 ft away. Captures "audio, notes, images and highlights." — [AffiliateBooster (low-quality source)](https://www.affiliatebooster.com/plaud-notepin-s-review/); [MightyGadget](https://mightygadget.com/plaud-notepin-s-review-vs-plaud-note-pro/)
- **Pebble Index 01 (ring):** "Press the button, whisper your thought, and it's sent to your phone." It records only while the button is held. Speech-to-text and an on-device LLM choose an action (create note, add reminder). It "never needs charging" (silver-oxide battery, about 2 years at 10–20 uses a day). iPhone and Android via the open-source Pebble app. Pre-order $75 (then $99), shipping from March 2026. — [repebble.com/index](https://repebble.com/index)
- **Sandbar Stream (ring):** "Push to talk & release to send" with haptic confirmation. Glass touchpad. Notes, reminders, to-dos and lists. "Whisper-to-text across any app" on iOS and Mac. Billed as "the private voice ring." Price not shown. — [sandbar.com](https://www.sandbar.com/)
- **Friend:** pendant with mic and Bluetooth, always listening. Replies come as texts to the phone. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friend_(product)); [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2025/10/01/who-is-avi-schiffmann-friend-ai-pendant-necklace)
- **Brilliant Labs Halo glasses:** $399, open source, with an AI agent ("Noa") that has "long-term memory of your life." Dual mics with audio activity detection and a low-power optical sensor. Shipping started early August (year not stated on page). — [brilliant.xyz](https://brilliant.xyz/products/halo)
- **Ray-Ban Meta:** "Hey Meta" wake word. Since Sept 2024 the glasses "help you remember things" (for example, your parking spot) and set timed reminders. — [Meta Newsroom, Sept 25 2024](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/09/ray-ban-meta-glasses-new-ai-features-and-partner-integrations/). With location enabled, you can see where a reminder was created. — [Engadget](https://engadget.com/wearables/metas-smart-glasses-can-now-tell-you-where-you-parked-your-car-195200826.html) (via search summary)
- **Rabbit R1:** handheld with screen. Now positioned as a computer-control and agent device. — [rabbit.tech](https://www.rabbit.tech/blog/first-major-update-of-2026-dlam-openclaw-and-a-surprise)

**Apps**
- **Voicenotes:** macOS, Windows, iOS, Android, watchOS, Wear OS and a Chrome extension. — [voicenotes.com/pricing](https://voicenotes.com/pricing)
- **AudioPen:** iOS/iPadOS app and keyboard, Android floating bubble, macOS and Windows hotkey, Chrome extension, web. Rewrites rambling speech into styled text. — [audiopen.ai](https://audiopen.ai/)
- **Letterly:** iOS, Android, Apple Watch, Mac, Windows, web. Inline dictation in Gmail, Slack, Notion, Telegram, WhatsApp, X and LinkedIn. Recordings up to 90 min. Claims 300k+ users, 4.8★ App Store and 4.7★ Play. — [letterly.app](https://letterly.app/)
- **TalkNotes:** web, iOS, Android. Records, then turns notes into 100+ style templates. — [talknotes.io](https://talknotes.io/)
- **Cleft:** iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch and CarPlay, plus widgets and Action button support. Transcribes on device. — [cleftnotes.com](https://www.cleftnotes.com/)
- **Superwhisper:** dictation into any app (⌥+Space on Mac). macOS, Windows, iOS, Android. — [superwhisper.com](https://superwhisper.com/)
- **Granola:** desktop, iOS, Android and Apple Watch. iOS records in-person meetings and outbound phone calls (calls are iOS-only). An active recording shows as a Live Activity on the Lock Screen and Dynamic Island. — [Granola docs](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/ios/getting-started); [Granola pricing](https://www.granola.ai/pricing). The Watch app (July 2026) can be set as a watch face to "start transcribing at any time," so you can "capture in-person meetings without having to take your phone out of your pocket." — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/28/granola-launches-an-apple-watch-app/)
- **Otter:** iOS and Android apps on paid tiers, Chrome extension, Zoom, Teams and Meet bots. — [otter.ai/pricing](https://otter.ai/pricing)
- **Notion AI Meeting Notes:** `/meet` on desktop. The mobile app records only what the phone mic hears and is best for in-person meetings. — [Notion Help](https://www.notion.com/help/ai-meeting-notes)
- **Mem 2.0:** "Turn on Voice Mode inside any note to capture a meeting or think out loud." Captures through "external channels such as WhatsApp." — [get.mem.ai/pricing](https://get.mem.ai/pricing)
- **Apple Notes:** audio recording with automatic transcription since iOS 18. Phone app call recordings save to Notes. Transcription needs iPhone 12 or later, and summaries need an Apple Intelligence device (iOS 26). — [Apfelpatient (secondary)](https://www.apfelpatient.de/en/tips-tricks/apple-notes-now-with-audio-recording-and-automatic-transcription); [Apple Support, Notes on Mac](https://support.apple.com/en-me/guide/notes/apdb5106e334)
- **NetNote:** "hit record after a meeting, talk naturally about who you met." One tap, speak about 30 s. — [netnoteapp.com](https://www.netnoteapp.com/)

### Inferences
- The lowest-friction triggers are hardware or OS-level: holding a ring or pin button, the Action button, watch face complications, Lock Screen widgets. A PWA cannot easily match the Action button, widgets or watch triggers. This argues for native shells, or at least iOS Shortcuts and home-screen icon deep links, early on.
- Hold-to-record (Pebble, Sandbar, Plaud highlight, Bee voice note) is the consistent pattern for short intentional notes. It avoids the social stigma of always-on recording.

### Gaps
- No product publishes measured time-to-capture (tap to recording). Letterly's "Talking is 5× faster than typing" is the only speed claim found, and it is marketing. — [letterly.app](https://letterly.app/)
- Sandbar Stream price and ship date were not on the fetched page. Pebble Index's actual shipping status after March 2026 was not verified.
- Bee's always-on versus button-only default is unresolved (conflicting sources).

## 3. Does it extract structured entities (people, phone numbers, birthdays, follow-ups), build a people/contacts memory, record location, or take photos?

### Takeaway
None of the major voice-note apps or wearables builds a structured contact record (name, phone, birthday, company, where met) from a spoken note. They produce summaries, to-dos and searchable transcripts. People-centric extraction exists only in (a) Bee's relationship/"Facts" learning and contact access, (b) Limitless's pre-meeting briefs by participant, and (c) a long tail of small indie personal-CRM apps (Keep AI, LynkIt, NexaLink, NetNote, WhispApp, Rel8, Ember), most with very few ratings. Geotagging people is rare: LynkIt has geotag plus map, and Meta reminders can show the location where they were created. This is the clearest white space.

### Cited Findings
- **Bee** "learns your patterns, preferences and relationships over time." It has a "Facts" section where users confirm or add personal info, suggested to-dos, and "Daily memories." It connects to Gmail, Google Calendar, phone contacts and Apple Health, and builds "knowledge graphs." — [bee.computer](https://www.bee.computer/); [TechCrunch hands-on](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/12/hands-on-with-bee-amazons-latest-ai-wearable/); [TechCrunch "Why Amazon bought Bee"](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/12/why-amazon-bought-bee-an-ai-wearable/). Bee "suggests LinkedIn connections." — [TechCrunch hands-on](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/12/hands-on-with-bee-amazons-latest-ai-wearable/)
- **Limitless** produced "pre-meeting briefs from past conversations with same participants." Speaker ID needed about 90 s of voice training. — [Fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/limitless-ai-review-2026/)
- **Omi** produces summaries, tasks, daily recaps and "searchable memory." No contact-record feature is advertised. — [omi.me](https://www.omi.me/)
- **Plaud** offers speaker labels, 10,000+ summary templates and "multidimensional summaries." No contacts feature is listed. — [Plaud pricing](https://www.plaud.ai/pages/plaud-ai-plan-pricing). The App Store privacy label lists Location as collected (not linked to identity). That is a data practice, not a feature. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/plaud-ai-notetaker/id6450364080)
- **Voicenotes, AudioPen, Letterly, TalkNotes, Cleft, Superwhisper:** transcription plus AI rewrite or structure (styles, bullets, action items). None advertises contact extraction, location stamping or person photos. — [Voicenotes](https://voicenotes.com/pricing); [AudioPen](https://audiopen.ai/); [Letterly](https://letterly.app/); [TalkNotes](https://talknotes.io/); [Cleft](https://www.cleftnotes.com/); [Superwhisper](https://superwhisper.com/)
- **Granola, Otter, Notion:** meeting-centric. They extract summaries and action items (Notion: "action items with owners"). Otter does speaker tagging. — [Granola](https://www.granola.ai/pricing); [Otter](https://otter.ai/pricing); [Notion Help](https://www.notion.com/help/ai-meeting-notes)
- **Mem** publishes a guide on using Mem as a "personal network CRM" with AI notes (positioning only, not a structured contacts product). — [Mem guide](https://get.mem.ai/guides/personal-network-crm-ai-notes)
- **Ray-Ban Meta:** camera glasses that can "remember" things like a parking spot. Reminders can show where they were created if location is on. — [Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/09/ray-ban-meta-glasses-new-ai-features-and-partner-integrations/); [Engadget](https://engadget.com/wearables/metas-smart-glasses-can-now-tell-you-where-you-parked-your-car-195200826.html). A face-identification feature ("Name Tag") has been reported but not launched (see Q5).

**Voice-to-contact long tail (directly closest to the new product's concept)**
- **Keep AI – Personal CRM (iOS, Björn Schefzyk):** dictate after a meeting. AI extracts "names, dates, follow-ups, and preferences." It has a business-card scanner, birthday and anniversary reminders a week ahead, relative-date parsing ("next Friday"), and profile fields for phone, email, role, industry, school, socials, birthday and partner. Export as Markdown history or CSV. "All data lives on your iPhone." $7.99/mo or $49.99/yr. 5.0★ from 4 ratings. Released Sept 27, 2025. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6751458016)
- **LynkIt – Networking Assistant (iOS):** card scanning extracts name, phone, email and title. Voice notes are transcribed onto contacts. Semantic search runs on a local vector index. "Geotagging" with an "interactive map" of your network. "Event Mode" auto-tags scans by conference. vCard export, address book import, company enrichment (logos, industry). Credits-based IAP ($0.99–$19.99). Too few ratings to display. Last update v1.9, Mar 25 2025 (possibly stale). — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6756911857)
- **NexaLink (iOS, WebMobi):** card scanning in 30+ languages, voice notes, AI-drafted follow-ups, Event Mode, smart follow-up reminders, Gmail sync, export or delete. $2.99–$4.99/mo, $49.99/yr, or $29.99 one-time. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6480664534). Its version dates looked inconsistent in the fetched listing, so they are unverified.
- **NetNote (Android, web):** a voice note becomes a summary, topics and promised follow-ups. Search by name, company or topic, and sort by event. The recording is kept. — [netnoteapp.com](https://www.netnoteapp.com/). A search snippet also claimed business-card OCR and dated follow-up tasks, which the official page fetch did not confirm. — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.netnote.app&hl=en)
- Also seen in search, not verified: WhispApp Personal CRM — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/whispapp-personal-crm/id6477359179); Rel8 AI (photo a card, LinkedIn enrichment, voice-note follow-ups) — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/rel8-ai); Ember (voice input, birthday reminders) — [Peerlist](https://peerlist.io/hactory1993/project/ember-relationship-keeper)

### Inferences
- The well-funded players (Plaud, Granola, Otter, Omi, Bee) optimize for meetings and ambient life logs, not "one person, one record." The indie apps that do structure people have negligible traction (single-digit rating counts). The field is open for a polished, fast, people-first capture tool.
- A geotag plus map plus city filter for people is effectively unclaimed. Only LynkIt (stale) has a map. Meta's "where you created the reminder" shows the idea is valued.
- Photos of the person, their business card and the place, combined with voice, is not offered as one flow by any notable product found.

### Gaps
- Could not verify whether Bee's "relationships" learning produces per-person profile pages or is only implicit.
- Could not verify whether Granola has people or company pages (not on the pricing page).
- No usage data for any of the indie voice-to-contact apps beyond App Store rating counts.

## 4. AI search/assistant, reminders, integrations (contacts, CRM, WhatsApp, Zapier), export, offline, languages

### Takeaway
"Ask your notes" chat is now table stakes (Ask Plaud, Granola chat, Otter AI Chat, Omi query, Bee search/chat, Mem agentic chat), as are MCP connectors to Claude and ChatGPT (Voicenotes, Plaud, Otter, Granola, Letterly, AudioPen). WhatsApp appears as an integration or capture channel in Voicenotes and Mem, but no WhatsApp-voice-note-to-contact pipeline was found. Offline capture is common on-device (Cleft, AudioPen, Superwhisper, Omi, Pebble, Mem 2.0). Language claims range from 15 (Notion) to 112 (Plaud). Arabic and Thai support was not explicitly confirmed for any product.

### Cited Findings

## 5. Privacy stance and consent features (recording others, on-device, retention, AI training), including facial-recognition controversies

### Takeaway
Two-sided privacy is the defining risk of this category. Vendors respond with "audio discarded after transcription" (Bee, Granola, AudioPen, TalkNotes), "never used to train AI" (Voicenotes, Letterly, AudioPen, TalkNotes), on-device processing (Pebble, Cleft, Keep AI, AudioPen offline) and explicit consent tooling (Notion; Voicenotes Enterprise). Limitless's promised "Consent Mode" never shipped. Meta's smart glasses are the lightning rod: the 2024 I-XRAY face-doxxing demo, the 2026 "Name Tag" face-recognition plan with a leaked memo about launch timing, LED-tampering mods, harassment filming, court bans and advocacy campaigns. Friend's NYC subway ads were defaced with "surveillance" graffiti.

### Cited Findings
**Vendor privacy postures**
- Bee "discards the audio after transcription," which makes playback for verification impossible. — [TechCrunch hands-on](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/12/hands-on-with-bee-amazons-latest-ai-wearable/)
- Granola deletes audio after transcription, offers a model-training opt-out per user or workspace, is SOC 2 Type II, and offers a DPA. — [Granola](https://www.granola.ai/pricing)

### Inferences
- A product where the user speaks about someone after meeting them, rather than recording the other person, avoids the consent problem entirely. That is a strong, honest privacy position: "we never record the other person; you narrate." Pair it with no AI training, an audio deletion policy and easy export.
- Photos of a person are a sensitive edge. Storing a photo the user took is fine. Any face matching or identification would put the product in the Meta "Name Tag" and I-XRAY controversy, so avoid face recognition and say so explicitly.
- Regulators and courts are now acting on wearables (court bans, FTC petitions). Phone-based, intentional capture is the lower-risk channel.

## 9. Synthesis: what the category teaches about capture UX and about social acceptability and privacy backlash

### Takeaway
Fast, intentional, one-handed capture (hold a button, speak, release) is winning over always-on recording. The wearables that recorded everyone (Friend, Humane, Limitless) either died, sold out to Big Tech or drew public hostility. Meta's face-recognition plans have become a regulatory flashpoint. The privacy-safe, socially acceptable pattern is "the user narrates about a person afterward." That is exactly the new product's concept, and it should be made explicit in positioning.

### Inferences
- **Capture UX lessons for a "people memory" app:**
  1. One gesture to start (ideally hold-to-talk with haptic or visual confirmation), no forms before speaking.
  2. Auto-stamp time and GPS silently, as Meta's reminders do.
  3. A fast, skippable review card showing extracted fields next to the playable original clip, so users can fix misheard names and numbers.
  4. Short notes (about 30 s, as NetNote suggests) beat long recordings.
  5. Native entry points (Action button, widget, Watch, Shortcuts, share sheet, WhatsApp forward) matter more than in-app polish.
- **Social acceptability lessons:**
  1. Never record the other person by default. Narration after the meeting is socially invisible and legally simpler than ambient recording, which needs two-party consent in some jurisdictions.
  2. Keep photos user-initiated and never run face matching. State "no face recognition" in positioning, given the Meta and I-XRAY backlash.
  3. Lead with "your data is never used for AI training," clear retention for raw audio, and one-tap export (vCard, CSV) and deletion. These are now the category's baseline trust claims.
- The "quiet utility, not a CRM" design goal matches the market. Heavy CRMs and meeting tools target teams, while capture-speed tools (Cleft, AudioPen, Pebble, Sandbar) win praise for minimalism.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data (time-to-capture, retention, abandonment) comparing triggers. All UX conclusions here are qualitative.
- No survey data on public attitudes toward voice wearables versus phone note-taking was found in this session.
- Two-party consent law specifics by country (relevant for any future "record the conversation" feature) were not researched here.
